SURVEYOR'S READING METHOD

More than a catalogue of future regulations

When I read a Future Legislation report as a surveyor, the priority is not memorizing every item number. It is identifying each requirement’s regulatory status, confirming its trigger dates and scope, and translating the text into documents, equipment, tests and operational evidence that can be verified on board.

This article uses the Containerships edition available from the official Lloyd’s Register Future IMO & ILO Legislation Summer 2026 page as its main reference. Search, status filters, a timeline and a newbuilding/survey checklist make the material usable for project briefings, design meetings, survey preparation and fleet regulatory tracking.

HOW TO READ THE REPORT

1. Report structure and usage

LR groups the regulatory status into four main parts:

  1. Part 1 | Requirements that have taken effect but are still in the transition period for full application
    These requirements are already in force, but may apply later to individual ships depending on ship type, tonnage, construction date, equipment installation date or the date of the first periodical survey.

  2. Part 2 | Requirements that have been adopted and will take effect in the future
    The IMO or ILO has adopted these requirements and confirmed their entry-into-force dates.

  3. Part 3 | Requirements under development
    These requirements have not yet been formally adopted. The report provides expected dates, but both dates and technical text may change.

  4. Part 4 | Proposed new requirements
    These items remain at the work-programme or early proposal stage; their scope and technical requirements are not yet settled.

The source report presents separate timelines for adopted and potential amendments. Its Figure 1 compares the greenhouse-gas fuel-intensity reduction paths for the IMO Net-Zero Framework’s Base Target and Direct Compliance Target.

EXECUTIVE TAKEAWAYS

2. Three-minute summary

2.1 Requirements most directly affecting container ships in 2026–2028

  • From 2026, shipboard lifting appliances and loose gear are governed by the new SOLAS II-1/3-13. Existing appliances must also undergo load testing and thorough examination and receive SWL markings and documentation by the specified renewal survey.
  • Fire-extinguishing foam containing PFOS is prohibited from 2026. Both new and existing ships must demonstrate removal by the first relevant statutory survey.
  • The Canadian Arctic and Norwegian Sea ECAs have been established. NOx Tier III applies to new ships meeting the construction-date criteria, while the 0.10% SOx limit applies from 2027.
  • From 2027, the NOx Technical Code adds recertification procedures for engines undergoing major conversion and requirements for multiple operational profiles and rational emission control strategies.
  • From 2028, new ships other than tankers of 20,000 GT and above must be fitted with emergency towing arrangements.
  • From 2028, pilot transfer arrangements are subject to comprehensive mandatory performance standards, including a phased upgrade schedule for existing arrangements.
  • IMDG Code Amendment 43-26 introduces provisions for lithium-metal batteries, sodium-ion batteries, vehicles and cargo transport units, with particular implications for container-ship cargo acceptance and dangerous-goods documentation.

2.2 Priority issues for advance planning in 2028–2032

  • IMO Net-Zero Framework There is no consensus yet, but if adopted by the end of 2026, it may enter into force in mid-2028 and would affect fuel selection, capital expenditure, operating costs and compliance costs.
  • The comprehensive review of the BWM Convention shifts the focus from equipment being installed and certified to its continued effective operation. Maintenance records, failure reports, repair plans, crew familiarization and annual sampling of active substances will become central to surveys.
  • The Phase 2 review of CII/EEXI has set tighter CII Z factors for 2027–2030. SEEMP may evolve from annual-rating management toward continual improvement and stronger tracking of company accountability.
  • Fire detection and control in container-ship cargo holds and on deck is expected to generate new SOLAS and FSS Code requirements around 2032, potentially covering infrared thermal imagers, fixed detection, remote-controlled water monitors, water-mist lances and hatch-cover protection.
  • Batteries as the main source of electrical power, non-conventional propulsion and steering systems, MASS, autonomous ships and firefighting for alternative fuels are all giving rise to new regulatory frameworks.

In force / transitional2022-04-01MEPC.324(75)

ITEM 350 Item 350 | 2024 Amendments to the IGF Code In force / transitional2024-01-01
In force / transitional2024-01-01MSC.458(101)
  • Adopted: MSC.458(101)
  • Entry into force: 2024-01-01
  • The amendments cover secondary enclosures for gas fuel piping, containment of liquefied-fuel leakage, explosion relief for exhaust systems, and separation of Type C fuel tanks from spaces of high fire risk.
  • Exhaust systems on new natural-gas-fuelled ships must have explosion-relief devices unless they can withstand the most severe overpressure resulting from ignition.

SURVEYOR PRACTICE

8. Container Ship Newbuilding and Survey Checklist

This section is a practical reading order based on the original document. It is a practical summary rather than a separate list of regulations from the original text.

8.1 Design and plan-approval stage

  • Check the building-contract, keel-laying and delivery dates so that a delivery delay does not unexpectedly trigger newer requirements.
  • Confirm the applicable EEDI Phase 3 design reduction and the subsequent EEXI/CII operational strategy.
  • For LNG and other low-flashpoint-fuel ships, verify the 2026 and 2028 IGF Code requirements for:
    • fuel containment and A-60/cofferdam
    • pressure relief
    • bunkering interface
    • ventilation
    • hazardous area classification
    • fuel supply redundancy
  • For new container ships of 20,000 GT and above from 2028, confirm the emergency towing arrangements.
  • Allow for the 2028 standards in pilot ladders, accommodation ladders and combination pilot transfer arrangements.
  • Track new requirements for container fires in 2032 to avoid adding fixed detection, water cannon or hatch protection to new ships late in the design.

8.2 Construction and equipment installation phase

  • Lifting appliances and loose gear: SWL, load tests, certificates, permanent markings and registration.
  • Fire-extinguishing foam: Confirm PFOS-free and take inventory of PFAS risks ahead of time.
  • IHM: MD/SDoC integrity, supplier information, shipyard preparation and delivery certification.
  • Water-level detectors: do not treat conventional bilge alarms as a substitute on multiple-hold container ships.
  • LNG fuel systems: bunkering-pressure compatibility, ventilation between DBB valves, gas detection, PRV discharge and hazardous areas.
  • Use the latest formats for shipboard IMO documentation, including DCS, SEEMP, BWMP and BWRB records.

8.3 Sea trials, delivery and initial survey

  • Complete test evidence should be retained for emergency towing, steering, reversing and stopping performance.
  • For pilot transfer arrangements, lifting appliances, life-saving appliances and fire-safety systems, verify consistency among the physical arrangement, operating procedures, certificates and maintenance intervals.
  • For BWMS, verify not only the commissioning test but also the maintenance regime, alarms, bypasses, failure reporting and contingency procedures.
  • Confirm that the IHM, IAPP, IEEC and Cargo Ship Safety Equipment/Construction certificate supplements match the equipment actually installed.

8.4 Operational ship management

  • PFOS foam removal and shore disposal records.
  • Retain the original mooring design concept, MBLSD, line identification and replacement records.
  • Maintain annual/periodical inspection, thorough-examination and load-test records, together with the loose-gear register.
  • ECA route planning: NOx/SOx applicable dates in the Canadian Arctic, Norwegian Sea, and Northeast Atlantic.
  • Assess how tighter 2027–2030 CII Z factors affect speed, routing, utilization and retrofit investment.
  • Before the 2028 BWM amendments, establish auditable records for maintenance, failures, repairs and crew familiarization.

IMPORTANT NOTES

10. Important notes

  1. The LR report notes that SOLAS amendments generally follow a four-year cycle, with the next principal entry-into-force date on 2028-01-01; exceptional items may still be advanced.
  2. New-ship requirements often use three concurrent thresholds—building contract, keel laying and delivery. A delivery delay can unexpectedly bring an original design within a newer regulatory regime.
  3. “All ships” means all ships within the scope of the relevant Convention, Annex or chapter—not every type of marine craft.
  4. Trading area, fuel type, ship service and equipment choices can all change actual applicability.
  5. Parts 3 and 4 summarize LR’s view of ongoing IMO/ILO work. Formal plan approval, survey and compliance decisions must use the latest resolutions, circulars, flag State instructions and classification rules.
